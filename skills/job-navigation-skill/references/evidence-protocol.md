# Evidence protocol

Use this file for academic, disputed, high-stakes, or multi-source research.

## Question and inclusion rules

Define before searching:

- decision and subquestions;
- population/context, intervention or option, comparator, outcome, and time horizon when applicable;
- geography and language;
- freshness window for each claim type;
- accepted source types and explicit exclusions.

Use the user's requested window for market signals. Do not apply a six-month cutoff to timeless theory or foundational models; retrieve the latest valid edition plus the original/authoritative source when useful.

## Source hierarchy

Use the best source for the claim, not one universal ranking:

1. Laws, standards, product behavior, company requirements: regulator, standards body, official documentation, company career page.
2. Causal or intervention claims: systematic reviews/meta-analyses, strong controlled studies, then observational evidence.
3. Labor-market scale: official statistics and representative surveys.
4. Current employer demand: recent, deduplicated job ads plus direct company postings.
5. Experience and implementation friction: practitioner reports and diverse first-person accounts, clearly labeled.

For every pivotal claim, seek either one strong direct source or two independent sources. Triangulate important recommendations across at least two source types.

Fit pivotal-claim breadth to the selected research budget:

- `quick`: normally support 2–3 pivotal claims;
- `standard`: normally support 3–6 pivotal claims;
- `deep`: normally support 5–10 pivotal claims, adjusting to the question.

Treat these as scope controls, not quotas. If the available source budget cannot support every planned pivotal claim, narrow or merge the claims, downgrade confidence, or disclose the gap instead of weakening the evidence rule. Exceed the nominal source range only when a consequential claim genuinely requires it, and explain the expanded scope.

## Lightweight appraisal

Score mentally; show only decision-relevant weaknesses:

- Authority: is the producer qualified and accountable?
- Directness: does it measure this population, geography, role, and outcome?
- Recency: is it fresh enough for this claim?
- Method: are sample, definitions, comparisons, and uncertainty disclosed?
- Independence: are incentives or conflicts material?
- Consistency: do independent sources converge?

Confidence labels:

- `High`: direct, recent, methodologically strong evidence with convergence.
- `Medium`: useful evidence with one important limitation or mixed findings.
- `Low`: indirect, sparse, self-reported, conflicted, or contradictory evidence.

Do not reuse these source-confidence labels as candidate-skill ratings. For career work, apply the separate A/B/C/D/U candidate-evidence grades in [career-module.md](career-module.md). A high-confidence labor-market claim can still reveal a weakly evidenced candidate skill, and vice versa.

## Search log for standard/deep work

Keep a compact internal table: query/theme, source, date, geography, claim supported, limitation. Report counts and exclusions only when they help the user assess coverage.

Use PRISMA 2020 principles for transparent reporting when conducting a review-like search. Do not say the work is a systematic review unless databases, search strings, dates, screening, exclusions, and synthesis meet that standard. Use GRADE terminology only for a body of evidence and explain adaptations outside health research.

## Contradictions

When sources disagree, test whether the cause is geography, period, role definition, seniority, sample, metric, incentive, or method. Preserve unresolved disagreement and explain how it affects the decision.

- PRISMA 2020 statement and checklist: https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020-statement
- GRADE Working Group: https://www.gradeworkinggroup.org/

- ILO statistics: https://ilostat.ilo.org/
- OECD employment data: https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/employment.html

These are starting points, not automatic evidence for every problem. Verify current pages at use time.
